# Statement of intent

*Quote this freely: in a GitHub or GitLab README, a Discord or Zulip intro, a mailing-list
post, or a future comment when someone asks what this is.*

---

I want to be clear about the intent here.

This is not meant as "please believe my MUB(6) paper," and it is not a claim to have solved
MUB(6). The result is one exact, checkable subproblem: the fourth-vector feasibility wall for
the canonical product triple.

What I would really like to help build is a community defect atlas for MUB(6): exact
pinned-triple walls, failed extensions, verifier bundles, formalized lemmas, SOS and
certificate attempts, Weyl-board structure, and carefully labeled numerical evidence.

The reason I think this matters is that MUB(6) has been hard partly because it is too easy to
generate plausible-looking false positives. A better path may be to break the problem into
small, independently checkable pieces that many people can audit or extend.

This record is one brick. If it holds up, I would love for others to help choose, verify, or
improve the next bricks: T1, parametric product triples, d=10, independent certificate
checkers, or Lean formalization. The goal is not for one person to own the problem. The goal
is to make the wall mappable.

That is the statement of intent I would stand behind.
